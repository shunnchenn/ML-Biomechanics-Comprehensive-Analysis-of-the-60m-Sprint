# The Leakage Ledger: What Survives Honest Cross-Validation in Sprint Kinematics

*MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference 2027 — Research Paper Competition (Other Sports)*

Motion capture and markerless pose estimation now let any team fit predictive models to athlete movement, and published sprint-kinematics models report strong accuracy — R² = 0.80 (Vellucci & Beaudette, 2023, n = 40) and R² = 0.92 (MIT Sloan, 2026). We show, on 30 sprinters (150 top-speed strides, 6.4–9.4 m/s, 64-marker capture at 60 Hz), that most reported accuracy of this kind is an artifact of validating a model while selecting its features on the full sample, and we quantify what each shortcut is worth.

Under leave-one-out cross-validation with every preprocessing and selection step refit inside each fold, kinematics predict peak velocity modestly but genuinely: knee range of motion plus stature reach LOO R² = +0.256 (RMSE 0.61 m/s; 5,000-shuffle permutation p = 0.002). The same procedures scored the common way inflate sharply. Selecting the top three features on all 30 athletes lifts R² by +0.156 (+0.234 for five). An exhaustive search over 1,540 models reports +0.403 but scores −0.020 once the search is repeated inside each fold — even though one model wins 26 of 30 folds, showing that a stable choice fit to noise is still noise. A 22-feature ridge lands below an intercept-only baseline.

Four diagnostics behave as controls rather than effect sizes. (1) Functional PCA's first component explains 42% of variance yet correlates −0.18 with speed; standardization hands it to the single angle with the largest between-athlete-to-within-cycle variance ratio (trunk lean), making the predictive knee invisible. (2) Stance-phase range of motion correlates with speed across seven joints; all seven vanish once the outcome-determined stance-window length is controlled. (3) Reliability (ICC) collapses from 0.80 to 0.36 as strides are pooled, because the added strides drift back into acceleration — the opposite of the assumed power gain. (4) A measurement audit confirms the headline variable is real: 60 Hz misses the knee-flexion peak by only 0.2°, uncorrelated with speed (r = −0.00), so a 16° gap versus published values is a definition offset, not a speed-dependent artifact, and the result is unchanged under a full 3-D definition (r = −0.62).

Finally, statistical parametric mapping with cluster-based permutation localizes where speed lives in the stride and exposes a segmentation bug: averaging alternate-foot acceleration steps blends stance and swing (phase scatter 90%); same-foot steps cut it to 14% and turn a fragmented, non-significant trunk-lean signal into a significant swing-phase effect (r = +0.53, p = 0.02).

The deliverable is a reusable leakage ledger — a reproducible notebook that prints every claim beside the cell that computes it, with data and code released openly. The contribution is not a better sprint model but an honest accounting any team can run before trusting one.
